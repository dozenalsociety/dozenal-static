Interactive Dozenal Chord Dialer

Note numbers are expressed in dozenal doublings of frequency with respect to C in octave 0. In other words, each note number is the binary logarithm of the ratio of its frequency to the frequency of C0, expressed in base twelve. The integer digit (whole doubling) encodes the octave number. The first fractional digit (uncia·doubling) encodes the semitone (when rounded). The second and third fractional digits (bicia·doubling and tricia·doubling) are the dozenal equivalents of cents. Middle C = 4.0z doublings. High C = 5.0z doublings. Low C = 3.0z doublings.

Drag the chord polygon to turn it; it snaps to the selected temperament's twelve note positions. The dot marks the root. The numbering updates at every snapped step, including notes that cross into the next octave (frequency doubling). Each complete clockwise turn doubles the frequency; counterclockwise halves it. Select a note below, or use ← and →. Hold Play Chord to sound the notes; release to stop.

Interactive adaptation of the Dozenal Society of America's Chord Dialier, by Donald "Troy" Hammond.
