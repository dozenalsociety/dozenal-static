Interactive Dozenal Chord Dialer

Note numbers are expressed in dozenal doublings of frequency with respect to C in octave 0. In other words, each note number is the binary logarithm of the ratio of its frequency to the frequency of C0, expressed in base twelve.

The integer digit (whole doubling) encodes the octave number. The first fractional digit (uncia·doubling) encodes the semitone (when rounded). The second and third fractional digits (bicia·doubling and tricia·doubling) are the dozenal equivalents of cents.

Negative octaves are indicated by a line over the whole doubling. However, the fractional digits are always positive.

Middle C = 4.0z doublings. High C = 5.0z doublings. Low C = 3.0z doublings. Supported notes range from C1 (1.0z doublings) to B↊ (↊.↋z doublings).

The polygon inside the dial depicts the chord or scale. The root note of the chord or scale is highlighed on the dial. Drag the polygon to rotate it. It snaps to the selected temperament's twelve note positions. The numbering updates at every snapped step, including notes that cross into the next octave (frequency doubling). Each complete clockwise turn doubles the frequency. Counterclockwise halves it.

The keyboard below also depicts the chord or scale. To pick a new root note, select any key on the keyboard, or use ← or →.

Press ▶️ to start playing the chord or scale. Press ⏹️ to stop. A scale plays upward and downward, one note at a time, with a beat of rest between directions.
